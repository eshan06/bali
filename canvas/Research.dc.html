What the research says about phones in class.

The strongest studies and surveys we found, and what each one measured.

The numbers

  • 72%

    of U.S. public high school teachers say phone distraction is a major problem in their classroom.

    Survey of 2,531 teachers
    Pew Research Center, 2024 (opens in a new tab)
  • 53%

    of U.S. public school leaders say phones have hurt academic performance, and 73% say attention spans.

    Survey of 1,490 schools
    NCES, 2025 (opens in a new tab)
  • Half

    of students still check a phone in class at least once a day, even at schools where phones aren't allowed.

    Survey of 922 students, grades 6 to 12
    RAND, 2026 (opens in a new tab)
  • 15 points

    lower in PISA maths for students who said classmates' devices distracted them in at least some lessons.

    About 690,000 15-year-olds. A link, not proof of cause
    OECD, PISA 2022 (opens in a new tab)
  • 75%

    of 317 Dutch secondary schools reported better concentration after the 2024 rule on phones in class.

    School leaders' reports
    Dutch Ministry of Education, 2025 (opens in a new tab)
  • 40+

    U.S. states, plus D.C., now require schools to restrict student phone use.

    See every state
    Education Week, Oct 2026

Studies worth reading

Phone-free lectures raised grades.

In a semester-long randomized trial with 16,683 university students in India, grades rose when students handed in their phones at the start of each lecture. Lower-performing and first-year students gained the most.

Peer-reviewedScience, 2026
Read the study (opens in a new tab)

Bans helped the students who struggled most.

After schools in four English cities banned phones, exam scores at 16 rose by about 0.07 standard deviations, almost all of it among the lowest achievers. A Swedish replication found no effect.

Peer-reviewedLabour Economics, 2016
Read the study (opens in a new tab)

Devices in the room lowered scores for everyone.

In a Rutgers course where devices were allowed in half the lectures, later exam scores on that material fell, even for students who never used one.

Peer-reviewedEducational Psychology, 2019
Read the study (opens in a new tab)

Florida: small gains, and a rough first year.

After Florida's ban, test scores rose 1.1 percentile points in the second year and unexcused absences fell, but suspensions rose in the first year.

Not yet peer-reviewedNBER working paper, 2025
Read the study (opens in a new tab)

Each number was checked against its source on November 6, 2026. Survey answers, school leaders' reports and correlations are marked as such, and none of these studies tested Bali itself.
